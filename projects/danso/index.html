Danso: an adaptive study partner for Ghana's NSMQ

A study platform for Ghana's National Science and Maths Quiz that retrieves real past questions, explains them with an open language model, and adapts which contests it quizzes you on as you improve.

Role
Builder of the retrieval, model serving and bandit

Why

Ghana's National Science and Maths Quiz (NSMQ) is a televised competition between senior high schools, with rounds ranging from fundamentals in biology, chemistry, physics and maths to speed races and a timed problem of the day. Schools with strong coaching and archives of past questions have a large advantage. Danso aims to give a student anywhere in the country the same access: real past questions, explanations on demand, and practice that adapts as they improve.

Architecture

How it works

  • A searchable archive. Past NSMQ questions, collected as spreadsheets by year and round, are embedded with the all-mpnet-base-v2 sentence transformer. Each question's preamble is joined to the question text so context is not lost, and a year-and-round feature is appended to the embedding so a search can be steered toward a particular contest. Everything goes into a FAISS index.
  • An open model on a small budget. Explanations come from Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.3 loaded in 4-bit NF4 with double quantisation through bitsandbytes, small enough to serve from a single Colab GPU behind FastAPI.
  • Structured requests. Commands like "show me a 2019 chemistry question" are first turned into a small JSON object (year, subject, round) by the model itself, which then drives the retrieval. Ordinary questions go to a tutoring prompt that includes the recent conversation.
  • Practice that adapts. A LinUCB contextual bandit treats each past contest as an arm. It balances quizzing the learner on contests where they are likely to improve with exploring ones they have not tried, and it updates after every answer.
