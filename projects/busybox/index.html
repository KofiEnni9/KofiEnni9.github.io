BusyBox: a benchmark for affordance generalization

A 3D-printable kit of buttons, switches, sliders, a knob and wires that snaps together into many different-looking devices, built to test whether vision-language-action models really understand the controls they manipulate.

Role
Robotics research intern: data-collection automation and robot operation
When
August 2025
Paper

The question

Robot foundation models, also called vision-language-action (VLA) models, can press a button or flip a switch they were shown during training. The open question is whether they learn the affordance, for example that a switch flips and a knob turns, or simply memorise where each control sat in the scene. BusyBox is a benchmark built to tell those two apart.

The hardware

BusyBox is a 3D-printable kit of six modules that snap together:

  • Display: an E Ink screen with LED indicators and the main electronics
  • Buttons: four coloured, illuminated buttons
  • Sliders: two adjustable sliders
  • Knob: a six-position rotary knob with a handle
  • Switches: two on/off switches
  • Wires: coloured wires with pluggable connectors

Modules can be swapped and rotated in under two minutes, so the same controls can be rebuilt into many devices that look different but work the same way.

The protocol

A model is fine-tuned on demonstrations from one "canonical" BusyBox layout and then tested on the same tasks with the box reconfigured. A model that has learned what a slider is should still work once the slider moves; a model that memorised the slider's position should not.

The released dataset has more than 1,000 language-annotated demonstrations collected by teleoperating a bimanual Mobile ALOHA robot. They cover seven task families and randomise the starting state of each control, the box's position and orientation, the robot's starting pose, and the wording of the instruction (by colour or by position).

The paper finds that affordance generalization is hard for even the strongest open-weight models it tests, π₀.₅ and GR00T-N1.6. Even "in-distribution" tasks can require out-of-distribution generalization in what the camera sees once the layout changes.
