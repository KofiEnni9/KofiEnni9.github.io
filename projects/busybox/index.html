BusyBox: a benchmark for affordance generalization

A physical board of switches, sliders, buttons and dials that can be rearranged into many different-looking devices, built to test whether vision-language-action policies really understand the controls they manipulate.

Role
Robotics research intern: data-collection automation and robot operation
When
August 2025
Paper
Fortier, Adamson, Hellebrekers, LaScala, Ennin, Murray, Kolobov, Mullins. arXiv:2602.05441. Accepted at CoRL.
Robots
Mobile ALOHA (bimanual), with π₀.₅ and GR00T-N1.6 baselines

The question

Vision-language-action (VLA) models can open a drawer or press a button they were shown during training. The open question is whether they understand the affordance, for example that a toggle switch flips and a slider slides, well enough to operate a device they have never seen that is built from the same kinds of controls. BusyBox is a benchmark built to measure exactly that.

The benchmark

BusyBox is a physical board made of six modules carrying switches, sliders, buttons, a dial and a display. The modules can be swapped and rearranged, so the same set of controls can be presented as many visually different devices. A policy that has really learned "how a slider works" should transfer across layouts; one that memorised a scene should not.

The paper evaluates strong open-weight VLA models, π₀.₅ and GR00T-N1.6, and finds that generalising across BusyBox variants is very hard even for them. To make the benchmark easy to reproduce, the team released CAD files, a bill of materials and a language-annotated demonstration dataset collected on a bimanual Mobile ALOHA robot.

My part

  • Automated the data-collection pipelines used to record demonstrations.
  • Operated the robotic arm to collect demonstrations for the dataset, which grew to 1,993 bimanual demonstrations.
  • Co-authored the paper.
